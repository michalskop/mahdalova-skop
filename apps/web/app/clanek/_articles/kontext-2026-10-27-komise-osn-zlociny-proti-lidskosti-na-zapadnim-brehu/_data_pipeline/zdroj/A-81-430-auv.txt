 I. Introduction, methodology and legal framework 
1. In this report, the Commission sets out its findings regar ding the drivers of Israeli and 
Palestinian state violence, in particular killings and bodily and mental harm, including sexual 
and gender-based violence. As part of a broader investigation into the root causes of recurrent 
tensions, instability and protraction of conflict, this report also reviews policies and practices, 
examining drivers of violence. 
2. The Commission applied the same methodology and standard of p roof previously 
adopted for its investigations and collected and preserved infor mation from a variety of 
sources, in accordance with its standard methodology of evidence preservation. 1 The report 
should be read in conjunction with the Commission’s previous reports and conference room 
papers. 2 
3. The Commission issued a call for submissions on 30 September 2025 soliciting 
information concerning killing and causing serious bodily or mental harm committed by any 
state or non-state actor since 7 October 2023. As part of its mandate to look into underlying 
root causes, the Commission also asked for information relating to incidents from the periods 
1948-1949, the 1950s, and the years 2000-2005. The Commission proposes to separately deal 
with this in its reports to be hereafter submitted. 
4. The Commission has sent two requests for information to th e Government of Israel, 
and two requests for information to the State of Palestine . The Commission received a 
response from Palestine but has not received any response from Israel. 
5. The Commission relied on the legal framework adopted thr oughout its work and 
reflected in its terms of reference. The Commission i s guided by the 2024 Opinion of the 
International Court of Justice on, inter alia, the effects o f Israel’s policies and practices on 
the exercise of the Palestinian people’s right to self-determination. 
 II. Killings and causing bodily and mental harm by Israeli for ces  
 A. Introduction 
6. The Commission carried out its analysis against the bac kdrop of the United Nations 
General Assembly Resolution 181 from 1947, which terminated t he British Mandate on 
Palestine and adopted the Partition Plan, dividing the terr itory into three parts: two States, 
one Arab and one Jewish, and Jerusalem under international control. The plan granted almost 
56 percent of the land to a Jewish state, Jews then being about one-third of the total 
population. The proposed Jewish state had an Arab population of about 47 percent. After the 
war of 1948, the newly established State of Israel occupied ove r 75 percent of the former 
British mandate. 
7. The State of Israel was established in May 1948 during what Is raelis refer to as the 
“War of Independence”, when 6,000 Jews (reportedly around 4,000 comb atants and 2,000 
civilians) were killed. The same war, referred to by Pal estinians as the Nakba  (“the 
catastrophe”), resulted in the killing of 13,000 to 15,000 Palestin ians (the majority being 
civilians), and the destruction and/or depopulation of about  530 Palestinian towns and 
villages.  Yishuv Jewish militia and Israeli military forces, including violent extremist Zionist 
militant organizations - the Irgun (Etzel) and Lehi (Stern G ang) - were the perpetrators of 
attacks against Palestinians civilians. 3 All Yishuv militias were later integrated into the 
regular Israeli army in May 1948, but some continued to operat e independently for some 
time. 
8. With central direction and authorization, Yishuv forces syste matically forced the 
Palestinian people from towns and villages with the int ention of ensuring a greater Jewish 
demography. Yishuv forces expanded Israel’s borders beyond what w as decided on in the

9. Already in October 1947, David Ben Gurion, then leader of the Yishuv, reportedly 
stated his intention to create a military force to repel p otential attacks and occupy as much 
land as possible. One year later, as Prime Minister, Ben -Gurion reportedly told the Cabinet

Palestinian population was necessary to securing the Je wish state, opposing a Palestinian 
state and blocking the exercise of the Palestinians’ right to self-determination. About 750,000 
Palestinians were displaced, including about 80 percent of the Arab inhabitants of what 
became Israel. They and their descendants continue living as refugees to date. 
10. The so called “maximum land minimum population” policy is a  continuous trend until 
today. In September 2025, the Minister of Finance, and additional  Minister in the Ministry 
of Defense with responsibilities for the West Bank, Bezal el Smotrich, insisted that a clear 
Jewish majority in a Jewish and democratic State of Isr ael will be preserved, stating that 
Israel must not seek sovereignty over a population that seek s its destruction: the overriding 
principle for applying sovereignty is: “maximum land with minim um population". Israeli 
officials have also repeatedly asserted that Palestinia n statehood is a threat to the existence 
of Israel and equated Palestinians with enemies of the State. The Knesset passed a resolution 
in July 2024 confirming that the establishment of a Palestinian state west of the Jordan river 
would pose a threat to the State of Israel and its citizen s, perpetuate the conflict and 
destabilize the region. 4 This was already reiterated in February 2024 by the Ministe r of 
Settlements and National Projects, Orit Strock, when he s tated in the Knesset that “…there 
will never be a Palestinian state in the land of Israe l, because there is no such thing as a 
Palestinian people ”, and because it would constitute “an existential threat t o the State of 
Israel and world peace”. 5 
11. The perception of Palestinians and a possible Palestinian st ate as a threat to Israel and 
the desire to reduce the Palestinian population contribut e to a permissive military culture 
where Israeli soldiers, with or without directives from comm anders, directly target 
Palestinians whether or not they pose a real or immediate threat. 6 Testimonies by Israeli 
soldiers in Gaza display a disregard for Palestinian lives  and a military culture where 
Palestinians are dehumanized, equated with “terrorists”,  trivializing the killing of civilians.  
From 7 October 2023 to 9 October 2025, over 72,600 Palestinians have been killed in Gaza 
and over 1,000 in the West Bank. From 10 October 2025 to 30 Septem ber 2026, 1,431 
Palestinians were killed in Gaza and 137 in the West Bank. 7

 C. Factual findings on the West Bank 
17. The number of Palestinian fatalities in the West Bank ha s significantly increased since 
2022, peaking in 2023 and 2024 with almost 500 Palestinians killed each ye ar - the highest 
number since OCHA data collection on this topic began in 2005. B etween 7 October 2023 
and 30 September 2026, 1,080 Palestinians (including at least 784 men, 237 boys, 22 women 
and seven girls) were killed and 10,940 were injured by Is raeli forces in the West Bank, 
including East Jerusalem. 18  
18. The rules of engagement of Israeli forces in the West Bank were reportedly broadened 
after 7 October 2023, resulting in more Palestinian civilians killed and injured. Israeli forces 
have increasingly broad discretion to use lethal force against a perceived threat, regardless of 
whether the target objectively poses an imminent and real danger. The commanding officer 
of the Army’s Central Command, Major General Avi Bluth, report edly stated in May 2026 
that Israeli soldiers are “killing like we haven’t since 1967”.19

21. For this report the Commission investigated seven cases, a nd documented six cases,

2023 and June 2026, 24  which are indicative of the more permissive rules of engagem ent. In 
these cases, the victims were unarmed and did not pose a threat; in the majority of instances,

Commission concludes that Israeli soldiers intentionally killed all the victims. 
22. An illustrative case of this is the killing of a Turkish -American woman, Ayşenur Eygi, 
on 6 September 2024, in conjunction with a protest in Beita against the outpost of Evyatar. 
The victim was peacefully observing the protest when s he was shot in the head at an 
approximately 200-metre range. While clashes erupted at the protest, the victim was shot 
after the clashes had largely subsided about 20 minutes prior. The victim was the only person 
directly shot. The responsible Israeli soldier reportedly raised a fist to a colleague, signalling 
success. 
23. In some cases, Israeli forces fired live ammunition at v ehicles carrying Palestinian 
families, including children, claiming that they accelerated towards the forces and constituted 
a threat, when there was no evidence to support such a claim.  In one example, on 5 June

the car when approaching the soldiers, raising his hands. Evid ence shows that an Israeli 
soldier fired two rounds using an Israeli‑manufactured X95 Tavor rifl e with 5.56×45mm 
ammunition into the car, without warning, shooting the baby in the  head. No medical 
assistance was provided to the family. According to the Israeli Army, a member of the 
Yehuda Brigade fired at the vehicle due to the car accelerating towards the soldiers. At a 10 
metre range, the soldier would have had a clear, unobstruc ted view of the persons in the 
vehicle. In a similar case documented by the Commission, Israeli forces fired at a car carrying 
a family of six in Tammun, Tubas Governorate, on 15 March 2026. The mother, father and 
two of their youngest sons were shot in the head and killed inside  the car, while two older 
sons were injured and subsequently assaulted by the soldiers.

residents, including the 14-year-old boy. According to reports, another 14-year-old boy was 
killed, and at least two others were injured in this incident.

 E. Legal analysis 
 1. Israel’s violations of international law in the West Bank 
42. In analysing each element of crimes against humanity, the Com mission has 
considered key factors in its conclusions: (i) the large numbers and status of victims killed, 
injured, detained or displaced; (ii) the strategies and methods of the Israeli forces, including 
mass destruction and targeting the civilian population; (iii) the locations of the operations in 
densely populated areas; (iv) the duration of the operations; (v) the expanded, permissive 
rules of engagement; (vi) the statements of the Israeli aut horities as to the purpose of the 
operations; and (vii) the authorities’ consistent practice of denying responsibility, blaming 
victims and granting impunity to perpetrators. 
 2. Crimes against humanity (chapeau elements) 
  A widespread or systematic attack directed against the civilian population 
43. The Commission finds that Israeli military operations in t he West Bank since 7 
October 2023 constitute a course of conduct that forms part of a widespread and systematic 
attack against the civilian population. The attack require s a high level of coordination and 
organised planning, indicating the existence of a policy to attack the civilian population. The 
Commission finds that the Israeli authorities utilised settler violence 48  to target the Palestinian 
civilian population, and intended settlers’ conduct to form p art of the ‘attack’ against the 
civilian population. In relation to Ayşenur Eygi, the Commission unde rscores that she was 
perceived by the Israeli soldiers as part of the targeted population; her killing was therefore 
part of the attack directed against the civilian population. 
44. The Commission finds that sexual and gender-based violence by the Israeli soldiers 
and settlers is instrumentalised not only as punishment against the individuals, but as part of 
a pattern of collective punishment to fracture, expel, humiliate and subjugate the Palestinian 
population in its entirety. 49  The Commission finds that the targeting of children by the Israeli 
soldiers and settlers in the West Bank is deliberate. 50  Similar to cases of sexual violence, the 
Commission finds that Israeli authorities knew and intended the conduct against children to 
form part of the attack against the broader civilian population. 
  State policy and nexus 
45. The Commission considers (i) the statements of Israeli aut horities; (ii) the pattern of 
militarised operations in the West Bank; (iii) the duration of the attack and the nature of the 
operations; and (iv) the status of victims and the number of casualties. The Commission finds 
that the targeting of Palestinian civilians across different locations in the West Bank indicates 
a high level of coordination and organised planning and is the product of state policy. At the 
same time, Israeli forces conducted large-scale and organise d operations, for the alleged 
purpose of counter-terrorism, and detained Palestinian civilians from the West Bank. 51  The 
Commission finds that the Israeli authorities intended to c ollectively punish the civilian 
population in the West Bank due to the attack of 7 October 202 3 in south Israel and the 
alleged support and association of the civilian populations to Hamas. The Commission finds 
that there is a clear nexus between the acts committed and the widespread and systematic 
attack. 
  Mens rea 
46. Considering (i) the widespread and systematic nature of the a ttack; (ii) the inferred 
policy of the state to commit such attack; (iii) the stateme nts of the Israeli authorities; (iii) 
the duration of the attack; and (iv) the authorities’ consistent practice of denying 
responsibility and blaming the victims, the Commission finds that the Israeli authorities and 
the security forces knew that the acts constituted part of an attack against the civilian 
population in the West Bank, and so intended them.

forces, settler violence constitutes a component of the attack against this civilian population. 52

of the attack. 53  The Commission concludes that the Israeli authorities and security forces have 
committed the crime against humanity of extermination against the civilian population in the 
West Bank since 7 October 2023. 
48. The Commission’s findings on extermination apply equally to the  crime against 
humanity of other inhumane acts. The Commission concludes that the Israeli authorities and 
forces have committed the crime against humanity of othe r inhumane acts, in particular 
causing serious injury to body and to mental or physical health against the civilian population 
in the West Bank since 7 October 2023. 
  Torture, rape and other forms of sexual violence 
49. The Commission finds that investigated cases of torture, repr oductive violence, rape 
and other forms of sexual violence of comparable gravity were not isolated incidents. Rather, 
these acts form part of the broader attack directed against  the civilian population. 
Furthermore, the Commission finds that the Israeli authoriti es support and encourage such 
acts. The Commission therefore concludes that the Israeli authorities and security forces have 
committed the crimes against humanity of torture, rape and other forms of sexual violence of 
comparable gravity against the civilian population. 
  Persecution 
50. The permissive rules of engagement in the West Bank are i mplemented in a 
discriminatory manner, applying exclusively to Palestinians. 54  Soldiers are prohibited from 
using lethal force against Jewish Israeli citizens in t he West Bank in a similar manner.  The 
acts were directed against Palestinian civilians in the West Bank on the basis of their 
nationality, race and ethnicity. The Commission therefore con cludes that the Israeli 
authorities and military forces have committed the crime against humanity of persecution 
against the civilian population in the West Bank since 7 October 2023, based principally on 
nationality, race and ethnicity, intersecting with gender and age.  Similar to Gaza, the 
Commission finds that, in the West Bank, Palestinian childr en are deliberately targeted on 
discriminatory grounds based on their age. 55

 III. Conclusions 
58. Since the 1947-48 war, Israel has continuously striven to expand territorially and 
displace Palestinians through violence. The intentional killi ng and harming of Palestinians 
have clear demographic and ethnic considerations that stem from Israel’s colonising policies: 
seizing maximum land while minimizing the Palestinian popul ation in relation to size, 
autonomy and viability. Such policies originate in historical practices and patterns of action 
and have resulted in the excessive and discriminatory application of lethal force, displacing 
and  killing Palestinians over several generations, causing life-long physical or mental harm

 G. Legal Analysis 
72. The conduct of Palestinian Authority against Palestinians in the West Bank is 
analysed within the context of international human rights law. 
73. In relation to the incident of 9 December 2024, the Commi ssion finds that the 
Palestinian security forces used excessive and unlawful lethal  force and wrongfully caused 
the death of one victim and the injury of another. As the acts of the Palestinian security forces 
are attributable to the State of Palestine, the Commission concludes that the State of Palestine 
has violated the right to life and the right to security of the person. The Commission also 
finds that the act against the child has further violated his rights under the Convention on the 
Rights of the Child, namely the rights to life, survival and development and the right to be 
protected from all forms of physical violence, including violence committed by state actors. 
In relation to the incident of 28 December 2024, the Commission received information that 
the victim was threatened for her perceived support of Palestinian armed groups; if verified, 
it would amount to a violation of her rights to freedom of expression and to hold opinion. 
 H. Conclusion 
74. Palestinian state violence has targeted and continues to t arget members of opposing 
Palestinian armed groups and repress Palestinian civil and political opposition. The 
Palestinian Authority continues to struggle with credibility and legitimacy, more so following 
Israeli attacks on Gaza and the West Bank. It targets Palestinians who voice dissenting civil 
and political views, including journalists and human rights defende rs, resulting in killings 
and physical and mental harm, leading to an increasingly diminished civic space. 82  
75. The Commission concludes that the Palestinian Authority ha s violated the right to life 
and the right to security of person. The Palestinian Authority has  also failed to conduct 
prompt, effective and impartial investigations into incidents of killings to establish the truth, 
and to ensure effective remedy, including financial compensation, to victims and their family 
members.  
76.  The Commission also concludes that in accordance with its previous findings, women 
journalists and protesters continue to be among those who are silenced through threats and 
violence. The Palestinian Authority must take all appropriat e measures to ensure women’s 
equal right to participate in civil society and public and political life in accordance with the 
Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women.83
