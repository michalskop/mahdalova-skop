## 2. Electorate 2026; Arab and Haredi shares

**CBS media release 332/2026, 21 Oct 2026 (OK, Hebrew PDF; numbers read from the PDF):** https://www.cbs.gov.il/he/mediarelease/DocLib/2026/332/24_26_332b.pdf
English title page: https://www.cbs.gov.il/en/mediarelease/Pages/2026/Number-of-Voters-in-Israel-Ahead-of-the-Elections-to-the-26th-Knesset.aspx
- Eligible voters for the 26th Knesset **according to the Central Elections Committee: ~7.3 million** ("more than 7.3 million" – Maariv, 21 Oct 2026). These include roughly 810,000 living abroad.
- CBS figure for eligible voters **living in Israel: ~6.5 million**, up 5.4 % (+333,000) since Nov 2022.
- Composition of those living in Israel:
  - Jews 5,017 thousand (77 %)
  - **Arabs 1,159 thousand (18 %)** – Muslims, Christian Arabs, Druze
  - Others 346 thousand (5 %)
  - **Haredim 684 thousand = 13.6 % of Jewish voters = 10.5 % of all voters**
- **Arabs are 22 % of the population but 18 % of voters** (younger age structure, plus non-citizen residents, mainly in East Jerusalem and the Golan). Haredim: 13.9 % of the population vs. 10.5 % of voters. Secondary confirmation: TOI liveblog, 21 Oct 2026, https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog_entry/some-7-3-million-israelis-eligible-to-vote-in-next-weeks-election-of-whom-810000-live-abroad/ ; JNS, 22 Oct 2026, https://www.jns.org/news/israel-news/cbs-age-gap-leaves-haredim-and-arabs-underrepresented-in-israels-electorate
- Haredi caveat: the release notes that only about 3 in 4 people classed as Haredi in the population registry are Haredi in practice (snippet, UNVERIFIED). i24 gives 766 thousand Haredi voters, which contradicts the CBS figure of 684 thousand; use CBS.

**2022 (SECONDARY, CBS via TOI, 27 Oct 2022):** about 6.2 million eligible voters living in Israel (CBS) vs. 6,788,804 per the CEC. **Arabs 17 %**, Haredim 11 %, Jews 77 % (Haredim included), others 6 %. https://www.timesofisrael.com/with-200k-new-voters-6-2-million-eligible-to-cast-ballots-in-next-weeks-election/
→ The claim "~17 % in 2022" is **confirmed**. For 2026 the share is 18 %.

Independent projection (Taub Center, Alex Weinreb, Aug 2026): ~7.561 million eligible by the end of Oct 2026. This is higher than the CEC figure because it is a model estimate. https://www.taubcenter.org.il/en/research/passing-the-threshold/

**Note:** the 18 % / 22 % shares are percentages of residents of Israel (6.5 million), not of the 7.3 million total including voters abroad.

## 5. Ra'am in the Bennett–Lapid government; Rabin 1992–95

- **First independent Arab party in a coalition:** Ra'am signed the coalition agreement on 2 Jun 2021; the government was sworn in on 13 Jun 2021 (60–59). TOI: "became the first independent Arab faction to join a governing coalition" (17 Jan 2022, https://www.timesofisrael.com/how-islamist-lawmaker-mansour-abbas-has-shaken-up-israeli-politics/ – OK).
  - Nuance: until 1977 coalitions included Arab satellite lists tied to Mapai. Hebrew Wikipedia accordingly says "the first Arab party in a coalition since 1977" (https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/מנסור_עבאס). The safe wording is **"the first independent Arab party"**.
- **Mansour Abbas's role:**
  - **Chair of the Knesset Special Committee for Arab Society Affairs** from 27 Apr 2021 (Hebrew and English Wikipedia, citing a Knesset press release – OK as secondary). He had earlier been Deputy Speaker of the Knesset (from 2020).
  - **Not a minister.** English Wikipedia has "minister without portfolio" in the infobox, but this is **not supported** by Hebrew Wikipedia, the deputy-minister list of the 36th government, or the press. Mako (2021): he "would not serve as a minister" (snippet).
  - Deputy minister: the coalition agreement guaranteed Ra'am a deputy-minister post. It was first planned in the PMO, which Abbas declined, then offered at the Housing Ministry (Srugim, 23 Jun 2021, OK: https://www.srugim.co.il/574292-ההצעה-החדשה-של-אלקין-למנסור-עבאס).
  - I found no source confirming that he took up the post (**UNVERIFIED**; Hebrew Wikipedia lists no such role). Recommended wording: "he held no ministry; he chaired the Knesset committee for Arab society affairs".
- **Coalition deal** (Arutz Sheva, 3 Jun 2021, OK: https://www.israelnationalnews.com/News/News.aspx/307400):
  - Ra'am was to chair the Interior Committee and the Special Committee for Arab Society, and get a Deputy Speaker post
  - NIS 30 bn integration plan for Arab society
  - NIS 2.5 bn against crime
  - NIS 20 bn over 10 years for infrastructure
  - TOI (24 Jun 2022, OK) confirms the 30 bn + 2.5 bn five-year plan: https://www.timesofisrael.com/as-elections-loom-arabs-take-stock-of-raams-year-long-experiment-in-government/
- **Length and collapse:**
  - Government in office 13 Jun 2021 → Bennett and Lapid announce dissolution on 20 Jun 2022 → Knesset dissolved 29/30 Jun 2022 → Lapid PM on 1 Jul 2022 (transition government until 29 Dec 2022). As a functioning majority, roughly 1 year (~12.5 months to the dissolution announcement).
  - Reasons: coalition whip Idit Silman (Yamina) quit in **April 2022** (60:60 deadlock); **Ra'am froze** its coalition and Knesset membership after the Temple Mount clashes (announced 17 Apr 2022; https://www.timesofisrael.com/raam-mulls-coalition-membership-freeze-amid-temple-mount-tensions/ – headline/snippet); in June 2022 the coalition failed to renew the emergency regulations extending Israeli law to settlers in the West Bank (opposed by Arab MKs and right-wing opposition MKs); defections by other right-wing MKs.
  - MEPC (OK): https://mepc.org/commentaries/dissolution-bennet-lapid-israeli-government/ ; Wikipedia 36th government (OK): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty-sixth_government_of_Israel
- **Rabin 1992–95:**
  - Hadash (3 seats) and the Arab Democratic Party/Mada (2) = **5 seats outside the coalition**. They supported the government and blocked no-confidence motions.
  - After Shas left in Sep 1993 (Oslo), Rabin had a **minority government of 56 MKs**, supported from outside by the Arab parties.
  - Source: Ron Gerlitz (Sikkuy), +972, 31 Oct 2015, OK: https://www.972mag.com/rabins-legacy-a-government-inclusive-of-all-citizens-not-only-jews/
  - Wikipedia 1992: "Hadash and the Arab Democratic Party also supported the government despite not being coalition members" (OK): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Israeli_legislative_election
  - The Hebrew term "גוש חוסם" (blocking bloc) is common in Israeli political commentary but did not appear verbatim in the sources I read.

## 10. Terminology, Druze, population share

- **CBS, 19 Apr 2026 (media release 117/2026, OK):** https://www.cbs.gov.il/he/mediarelease/DocLib/2026/117/11_26_117e.pdf
  - Population 10.244 million: Jews and Others 7.790 million (76 %), **Arabs 2.157 million (21.1 %)**, foreigners 296,000 (2.9 %).
  - Official definition: "Arabs: Moslems, Arab Christians and Druze". **The CBS includes Druze among Arabs** and also counts East Jerusalem residents (mostly non-citizens) and the Golan Druze.
  - So 21 % of the population ≠ the share of citizens. For voters, use 18 % (item 2).
- **Official vs. self-identification:**
  - Israeli state usage: "Arabs" / "Arab citizens of Israel" / "Arab society" (e.g., the Knesset's "Special Committee for Arab Society Affairs").
  - Many Arab NGOs and academics use "Palestinian citizens of Israel"; Al Jazeera writes "Palestinian citizens of Israel, who number about 1.2 million" (voters).
  - Konrad Adenauer Program surveys (OK) on the primary component of personal identity:
    - May 2026: Arab identity 33.3 %, Israeli citizenship 27.7 %, religion 24.5 %, **Palestinian 13.5 %**
    - Nov 2025: 35.9 / 31.7 / 17.3 / 14.7 % (https://www.kas.de/en/web/israel/single-title/-/content/an-in-depth-survey-of-the-arab-community-in-israel-following-the-israel-hamas-ceasefire)
    - Dec 2024: citizenship 33.9 %, Palestinian 9 % (snippet).
  - → A good argument for neutral wording, e.g., "Arab (Palestinian) citizens of Israel", with a note that people describe themselves in different ways.
- **Druze:** Druze men (and Circassian men) have been conscripted since **1956** (at the request of Druze leaders). Women are exempt. Muslim and Christian Arabs are not conscripted but may volunteer. Wikipedia "Conscription in Israel" (OK; secondary, sources: NYT 2012, Ben-Dor 1979): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscription_in_Israel
  - Rudnitzky's turnout figures cover "Arab and Druze" localities together; Druze turnout 2021–22 averaged 48.7 % (IDI 47986).

### Main discrepancies (summary)
1. **2026 electorate:** CEC 7.3 million (including ~810k abroad) vs. CBS 6.5 million residents vs. Taub 7.56 million (projection). The Arab share of 18 % refers to residents.
2. **2022 Arab share = 17 %, 2026 = 18 %**, both CBS, of voters living in Israel.
3. **"66 %" (Knesset RIC)**: the source says "about two-thirds". The primary Knesset PDF is not accessible.
4. **Number killed**: Abraham Initiatives vs. Haaretz differ by 1–12 per year. 2022 especially: 108 / 116 / 120.
5. **Suicides 2025**: IDF 21 vs. Haaretz 22. The 2026 figure of 21 is from Haaretz.
6. **Abbas "minister"**: English Wikipedia's infobox is wrong. He was not a minister; the deputy-minister post is unconfirmed; he chaired the Arab society committee.
7. **Negev Bedouin**: 100,000 (Bimkom 2025) vs. 150,000 (RCUV, broader definition).
8. **NEET**: "one in three" holds for women (34 %); men 25 % (State Comptroller, 2021 data).
9. **National turnout 2021**: Rudnitzky 67.2 % vs. CEC 67.44 %.
10. **93 % of land**: managed by the ILA (in trust), not owned; JNF owns ~12 %.
